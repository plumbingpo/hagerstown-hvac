Why Hire a Professional for HVAC Repair?

Two things make HVAC repair a poor DIY project: the diagnosis is deceptive, and the equipment is dangerous. On the diagnostic side, a weak capacitor, a low charge, a frozen evaporator coil, and choked airflow can all produce a system that runs without cooling, so trial-and-error part swaps get costly in a hurry. On the safety side, you are dealing with 240-volt circuits, capacitors that stay charged after the breaker is off, federally regulated refrigerant that requires recovery equipment, and, on gas furnaces, fuel lines, combustion, and venting where one mistake can put gas or carbon monoxide into the home.

A trained technician with proper test equipment gets to the answer faster and more safely than trial and error ever will. Ours check capacitors and motors with a meter, look at superheat and subcooling before anyone opens a refrigerant line, confirm flame signal before a control board is blamed, and measure static pressure when the ductwork might be the real bottleneck. You also get an honest opinion on the equipment. If your system is near the end of its service life, you will hear it from us and help you decide between repair and replacement before money changes hands.

HVAC Repair Prevention Tips

A little routine maintenance goes a long way toward avoiding a mid-season breakdown. Follow these tips from our experienced technicians:

  • Change the filter every one to three months so the blower and coil never starve for air
  • Have the system professionally serviced twice a year, once before summer and once before winter
  • Ease the strain in extreme weather by nudging the thermostat a few degrees toward the outdoor temperature
  • Avoid closing or covering registers; the system is designed to move air through all of them
  • Take new noises seriously; a squealing motor bearing is far cheaper before it seizes
  • Run a vinegar flush through the condensate line each spring to prevent clogs and float switch trips
  • Trim shrubs and clear leaves and grass away from the outdoor unit so the coil gets full airflow
